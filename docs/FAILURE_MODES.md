# Failure modes

Use this document when a chapter “sounds fine” but fails to pull the reader
forward.

## Structural failures

- The opening explains a world before anyone wants anything.
- The protagonist reacts but never chooses.
- The obstacle vanishes because another character explains or gifts the answer.
- Victory changes no state and creates no new pressure.
- A hook is only a noun, title, prophecy, or vague conspiracy.

## Emotional failures

- The chapter asks for shock without building pressure.
- The payoff is only the narrator saying the protagonist feels good.
- Humiliation or oppression is repeated without escalation.
- Every scene uses the same emotion, so the reader cannot feel movement.

## Continuity failures

- Characters know information they have not learned.
- Injuries, time, distance, money, or items reset silently.
- New ability rules appear exactly when needed.
- A rejected candidate idea slips into canon.

## Prose failures

- Paragraphs become equal-length explanation cards.
- Dialogue delivers lectures instead of strategies.
- The narrator explains the reader's feeling.
- Repeated contrast sentences create a machine-like rhythm.

## Market failures

- The project follows a visible trend without a different mechanism.
- A title or opening overlaps too closely with a near neighbor.
- A single snapshot is treated as a trend.
- A platform outcome is promised without evidence.
